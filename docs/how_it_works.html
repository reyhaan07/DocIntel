docintel · how it works

How docintel turns a page into connected facts

docintel reads a document the way a careful clerk would: it finds every word and where it sits, works out which words are labels and which are answers, groups the answers into the people and things they describe, and then links those things to one another. Every link says why it exists, points at the spots on the page that prove it, and says how sure it is. Nothing is guessed: a link it cannot prove is kept and marked uncertain.

the pipeline

Five stages, each with one job

1

Read

Words with their exact boxes: from the PDF's text layer, its fillable form fields, or OCR for scans and photos (straightened, lighting evened, several passes that vote).

tokens · box · source · confidence
2

Locate

Headings by size, weight and spacing cut the page into sections; each is matched to a known section by its name and aliases. A section can run onto the next page.

regions · headings · lines
3

Bind

Labels are paired with their answers by layout, and the answers grouped into typed entities: a patient, a medication, a doctor, a policy.

entities · attributes
4

Relate

Rules turn layout facts (same row, same section, a ticked box, a matching name) into typed links, each with its reason, evidence and confidence.

relationships · basis · evidence
5

Validate

Nine checks re-examine the result: every value on the page, one row per medication, one owner per condition, one agreement per signature.

checks · review queue · missing

Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OpenDocument, HTML, e-mail, text, CSV and JSON files are first laid out as a page, so every format goes through the same five stages.

basisA sentence that names the layout fact the link rests on: the same row, the same section, a ticked box, a matching name.
evidenceThe boxes on the page that prove it, each with its role: the subject, the object, their printed labels, the section heading. Click the link in the app and they light up.
confidenceThe weakest of the readings involved. A link is never surer than the words it was built from.
supportexplicit when the evidence is complete; uncertain when any warning was raised or the confidence falls below the review threshold. Uncertain links stay in the result and go to the review list.
warningsWhy a link is doubtful, in words a reviewer can act on: "policy holder 'A. Raman' does not match spouse 'Arjun Raman'".

Answers are then grouped into entities of a known type. Some entities are derived: a doctor from a medication's "Prescribing Doctor", an insurer from a policy's carrier, a hospital from a surgery's facility. Each distinct name becomes one entity, so two medications from the same doctor point at the same doctor.

Raman") is not enough, and "Provider 1" never matches "Provider 2". Any link whose confidence is below the review threshold set by the form's template is also marked uncertain, whatever its rule.

stage 5 · validate

Nine checks before anything is trusted

  • Evidence provenance. Every traced value points at content that exists in this file, on a real page.
  • Predicate signatures. Every relationship has an allowed type between allowed entity types.
  • Medication record integrity. Each medication keeps the values of exactly one table row.
  • Insurance policy separation. Values of one policy never come from another policy's region.
  • Family history separation. Each condition has exactly one owner: the patient or one family member.
  • Consent decision integrity. Each consent has one resolved answer that matches its permissions.
  • Signature agreement binding. Each signature belongs to exactly one agreement.
  • Text coverage. Every piece of text on the page is accounted for: a value, a label, a heading or instructions.
  • Cross-field consistency. Values describing the same fact agree: the holder's birth date, ZIP and state, a birth date before every signature date.
the principle

A wrong answer that looks right is the one to prevent

docintel counts a silent error as a value or link that is wrong and still marked valid. A flagged error costs a reviewer one click; a silent one costs the business. So whenever the evidence is weaker than usual, the result goes to review instead of being trusted:

Doubtful OCR

A word read with low confidence, a letter that may be a digit (O/0, l/1).

Weak pairing

A value far from its label, printed in the label's own size, spread across the line like check-box choices, or carrying another label inside it.

A grid that is not a table

On a scan, cells that do not line up under their headings: most often a form read as a grid.

Words the scan lost

A row that leaves a column empty where the other rows fill it; a question read only in part.

Arithmetic that does not add up

A total below its tax, a subtotal above its total, cash minus change that misses the total. When two sums agree exactly, the reading is confirmed.

Implausible facts

A date in the future, a due date before the invoice date, a birth date after a signature date.

any document

Documents without a known form

Most documents have no template. docintel then reads their structure on its own (sections, labelled values, tables, check-box questions, signatures and running text) and links each to its section with CONTAINS. It names the document type from its title and wording (28 types: invoice, receipt, bank statement, payslip, discharge summary, contract, letter, e-mail …) and pulls that type's key facts: an invoice's number, dates, seller, buyer and total; a receipt's merchant, date and total; a bank statement's account and balances. Each key fact keeps its source box and its review status.
